# 3rd Edge Creative — Website Restructure Brief

*Content, positioning, and design system direction for the rebuilt 3rdedge.co.za*

---

## 1. Why This Rebuild

The current site is written for the wrong audience and in the wrong voice. It pitches "revenue engines" and "UX debt" in the language of a Silicon Valley growth-marketing agency, while 3rd Edge's actual client base spans government, international NGOs, and growing private companies — buyers who care about trust, credibility, and things working properly, not conversion-rate jargon.

This brief resets the site around a plainer, warmer, more confident voice; a tighter four-page structure; and a design system that gives the existing brand colors real discipline instead of using them all equally.

---

## 2. Voice Principles

These apply to every page, and to future content (blog posts included):

1. **Talk like a person who knows what they're doing, not a brand.** No "we leverage," no "ecosystems," no "industrial-strength." If you wouldn't say it out loud over coffee, it doesn't go on the page.
2. **Warm, but earn the warmth with specificity.** Warmth without proof reads as fluffy. Back it with something real and specific.
3. **Confidence stated plainly, not performed.** Say the true thing simply — let the plainness carry the confidence.
4. **Short sentences do the heavy lifting.** Long compound sentences are where jargon hides.
5. **Specific nouns over abstract nouns.** "Websites," "forms," "case management systems" beat "digital ecosystems," "journeys," "touchpoints."

**Target audience:** a broad mix — institutional buyers (government, NGOs) and growing private/fintech companies alike. Copy should not narrow to one or the other; it should speak to the shared want both groups have — trusting that the people building their site know what they're doing.

**Structural principle:** avoid generic agency-template patterns — two-column problem/solution grids, numbered four-step icon process cards, and stat-bar "flex" sections are all cut or replaced with more narrative, less templated structures throughout.

## 5. About Page

### Our Story — "A rare combination, on purpose"
3rd Edge Creative started with a simple observation: most people are good at design, or good at engineering — rarely both, and rarely well enough to move fluently between the two. We built the agency around closing that gap. We design and build, end to end, without a handoff where ideas get lost in translation.

Even now, with AI reshaping how software gets made, that combination is still surprisingly rare. Understanding both sides — how something should feel and how it actually gets built — is still what lets us deliver applications that work in practice, not just in a mockup.

### How We Think (three moments — replaces two generic feature grids)

**The brief becomes a build**
Normally, a designer says something like "make it feel effortless," and that gets handed to a developer as a ticket — and something gets lost in that handoff, every time. When the same people carry the idea from concept to code, "effortless" doesn't need translating. It just gets built that way.

**The edge cases show up early, not late**
A design file shows the happy path. The awkward cases — a form with no data yet, a name that's too long for its box, a slow connection — usually only surface once a developer starts building, by which point they're expensive to fix. We hit those the same week we're still deciding how something should look, while it's still cheap to change.

**The last 10% doesn't get skipped**
Polish is usually the first thing dropped when a project runs over budget or handoff friction eats the schedule — it's the part that depends on someone downstream still caring after the "real work" is done. When design and build live with the same people, that 10% isn't a nice-to-have someone has to fight for. It's just how the work finishes.

## 9. Design System

### 9.1 Color Palette

Brand colors (from the logo): orange **#EA5807**, slate grey **#6A727F**, rich yellow **#FFCC00**, plus an existing dark grey. The palette below keeps all of these but applies real hierarchy via the 60/30/10 rule, rather than using them with equal weight.

| Role | Color | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| **Dominant (60%)** | Dark charcoal **#14161A** (derived from the slate grey's cool undertone) | Primary background — dark-forward design for a premium, editorial feel that also suits scroll-driven motion |
| **Secondary (30%)** | Slate grey **#6A727F**, with tints/shades: lighter **#A8AEB6**, darker **#4A5158** | Body copy on light sections, secondary UI, borders, dividers, muted text on dark backgrounds |
| **Primary accent** | Orange **#EA5807** | Buttons, links, key CTAs, hover states — the color literally named after the agency; should own "action" |
| **Rare highlight** | Yellow **#FFCC00** | Small decorative details only — icon fills, underlines, a single emphasized word. **Never used as a text color** (fails WCAG contrast on both light and dark backgrounds) |

**Accessibility notes:**
- Orange (#EA5807) passes contrast for large/bold text and UI components on both white and dark backgrounds, but fails for small body text at standard weight on white — reserve it for buttons, links, and large type.
- Yellow (#FFCC00) should be treated as a graphic accent only, never behind or as body text.
- The dark-forward base makes white/light-grey text reliably AA-compliant throughout, and gives the orange accent more usable room than a white-background layout would.

### 9.2 Typography

- **Headline: Unbounded** (Google Font) — bold, geometric, used for H1/H2 and short display statements only. Gets visually muddy at small sizes, so it should not be used below ~24px.
- **Body: Montserrat** (Google Font) — handles everything from body copy to UI text, captions, and subheads where needed.

**Type scale (8-point grid aligned):**

| Level | Size / Line-height |
|---|---|
| Body | 16px / 24px |
| Subhead | 24px / 32px |
| H3 | 32px / 40px |
| H2 | 48px / 56px |
| H1 / Hero | 64–96px, fluid/clamp on desktop, scaling down on mobile |

### 9.3 Spacing & Grid

- **Base unit:** 8px
- **Common steps:** 8 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 48 / 64 / 96 / 128px
- **Section padding (desktop):** 128px top/bottom for major sections; 64px for sub-sections. Generous whitespace signals premium quality — cramped sections read cheap.

### 9.4 Motion

Motion should reveal information or make a transition feel intentional — never decoration for its own sake (which reads as templated rather than crafted).

1. **Hero:** subtle, ambient Three.js particle field or gradient mesh reacting gently to cursor movement against the dark background — atmosphere, not a party trick.
2. **Diagnosis-narrative section:** GSAP ScrollTrigger text reveal, one phrase illuminating at a time — motion in service of the copy's pacing.
3. **Process narrative:** a connecting line/SVG path that draws itself as the user scrolls, visually tying the single-paragraph narrative together.
4. **Case studies:** image reveals / gentle parallax on scroll to present real visual work.
5. **Micro-interactions everywhere else:** button hovers, link underlines, state-change easing — fast (150–250ms), never bouncy or elastic.

**Explicitly avoid:** parallax on every section, multi-directional text-fly-ins, scroll-jacking. These read as template effects and are frequently the first things to break accessibility.

### 9.5 WCAG Checklist

- Color contrast verified per the palette notes above (orange/yellow never used as small body text)
- `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback for every GSAP/Three.js animation — shows the end state directly
- Keyboard navigability maintained through any custom scroll-triggered sections — no focus traps, no motion-only affordances
- Alt text and semantic HTML maintained under any animated/JS-heavy implementation
